An additional card (tarjeta adicional) is a credit card your bank issues to someone else, at your request, on your own credit line. It has no limit of its own: what it spends comes out of the same credit limit as your card and shows up on your same statement.

That is why the additional card's debt is yours. The primary cardholder (titular) is the one who answers to the issuer for what is bought with it.

## The four concepts, simply put

- **Primary card (tarjeta titular):** the card issued to you, the owner of the account and the credit line.
- **Additional card (tarjeta adicional):** the card issued to someone else, such as your partner or a child, on your same line.
- **Total credit limit (cupo total):** the credit the bank approved. The primary card and every additional card share it.
- **Available credit (cupo disponible):** what is left of the total limit after what has been used. It goes down with every purchase on any of the cards and back up when you pay.

## An example with amounts

Say your total credit limit is **$1,000,000 CLP** and you asked for an additional card for your son.

| Card       | Purchases this month | Credit used so far | Available credit |
| ---------- | -------------------- | ------------------ | ---------------- |
| Primary    | $300,000 CLP         | $300,000 CLP       | $700,000 CLP     |
| Additional | $150,000 CLP         | $450,000 CLP       | $550,000 CLP     |

Both cards see the same available credit: **$550,000 CLP**. If your son spends $100,000 CLP more, you have $450,000 CLP left even if you bought nothing.

When you pay the statement, the credit frees up for both cards at once. There is no payment "for the additional card": you pay the account.

## A cap for the additional card

Many issuers let you set a **cap** (tope) for the additional card, lower than the total limit. Check your contract or your bank's app to see whether yours does.

With a cap of **$200,000 CLP**, your son could spend only $50,000 CLP more in the example; the bank declines purchases that go over it. That keeps the additional card's spending in check, but it still comes out of the shared limit: the cap does not give it a separate limit.

## What the regulation says

The credit card consumer information regulation in force today dates from 2012 (Decreto 44). It already says that the rights and obligations of an additional card are exercised by the primary cardholder.

The new regulation (Decreto 75) takes effect on February 6, 2028. It defines an additional card as one issued to a third party, at the cardholder's request, on the same credit line.

It also requires the statement to show, for each purchase, whether the primary or the additional card made it, with a total for each.

## Before you ask for an additional card

- **Trust:** any purchase on the additional card is your debt, and a late payment affects your credit history.
- **Costs:** some cards charge a fee for each additional card. It is in your contract and in the summary sheet (hoja resumen).
- **Cap:** if your bank allows it, set one from the start, so one large purchase does not use up your limit.

## How to do it in _fik_

In _fik_ the cards of an account share the credit limit just like at the bank, and you can see how much each one spent:

1. **Add your card:** create your credit card under **Accounts** with the total limit your bank approved. That first card becomes the primary one on its own, and its limit is the account's.
2. **Add the additional card:** on the same account, use **Add card**, turn on **Additional card** and type the name of the person who uses it in **Cardholder**.
3. **Choose its limit:** keep **Account pool** so it shares the whole limit, or pick **Own limit** to cap it. That cap can never exceed the account's limit.
4. **Record each purchase with its card:** when you record a purchase, pick the card that made it. The account's credit used adds up both cards, and tapping a card shows only its movements.
5. **Pay the account:** paying the statement with **Pay** frees the limit of every card, just like at the bank.
