If your card has an international credit limit (cupo internacional), what you buy in dollars is charged on a dollar statement, separate from the peso one. You can pay that statement in dollars or in pesos, at the exchange rate your bank uses on the day you pay.

Your bank's exchange rate does not have to be that day's dólar observado. That is why it is worth checking the conversion before you pay.

## The four concepts, simply put

- **International credit limit (cupo internacional):** the dollar credit your bank approved for buying or withdrawing outside Chile and on foreign websites. It is separate from the peso limit; the regulation calls it cupo internacional o en moneda extranjera.
- **Dollar statement (estado de cuenta en dólares):** the monthly document with your dollar purchases, its total to pay and its due date.
- **Exchange rate (tipo de cambio):** how many pesos the bank charges you per dollar when you pay in pesos. Your bank sets it.
- **Dólar observado:** the reference dollar rate the Central Bank of Chile publishes every day. It is useful for comparing, but the bank is not required to use it.

## An example with amounts

Say your card has an international limit of **US$1,000** and this month you bought in dollars:

| Date       | Purchase         | Amount     |
| ---------- | ---------------- | ---------- |
| October 4  | Online store     | US$120     |
| October 12 | Hotel on a trip  | US$380     |
| **Total**  | Dollar statement | **US$500** |

If you pay in dollars, you pay **US$500**. If you pay in pesos and your bank uses a rate of $950 CLP per dollar, you pay **$475,000 CLP**.

At $940 CLP per dollar you would pay $470,000 CLP. A $10 difference per dollar already adds up to $5,000 CLP in this example.

## Paying in dollars or in pesos

- **In dollars:** if you have a dollar account, you can pay from it and avoid the conversion.
- **In pesos:** the bank converts at its rate on the day you pay. You see it before confirming the payment in your bank's app or website.

Either way, paying the total on time works the same as in pesos: with most cards, single-payment purchases accrue no interest.

## If you do not pay the dollar statement

Some banks move the dollar debt you did not pay into pesos, converted at their rate, and add it to your next peso statement. Check your contract to see how yours does it.

From then on the debt is in pesos, with your card's peso interest.

## Fees for purchases abroad

Many cards charge a fee for each purchase or withdrawal abroad. It is in your contract and in the card's summary sheet (hoja resumen).

## What the regulation says

The credit card consumer information regulation in force today dates from 2012 (Decreto 44). It lets a bank card's limit separate the credit in pesos from the credit in another currency.

The new regulation (Decreto 75) takes effect on February 6, 2028. It requires a card with an international limit to have an additional statement every month, with a **"Monto Moneda Origen"** (original currency amount) column: if you bought in euros or yen, you will see each purchase's original amount.

It also requires showing the exchange rate applied when a purchase abroad is charged straight to the peso limit, and fees for purchases abroad to be reported in the foreign currency.

## How to do it in _fik_

In _fik_ your card keeps the peso limit and the dollar limit separately, just like your two statements:

1. **Turn on the dollar:** in **Profile → Preferences → Currencies and amounts**, add USD under **Extra currencies**.
2. **Add your international limit:** on your credit card, under **Limits in other currencies**, enter your dollar limit.
3. **Record your dollar purchases:** record them with the card and in dollars. Under **Statements** you will see one tab per currency.
4. **Generate the statement:** when you generate it with your statement's dates, the peso period and the dollar period close together.
5. **Pay the dollar statement:** on the USD tab, use **Pay**. Enter what you paid in dollars and, if you paid in pesos, what left your account: _fik_ suggests it with the dólar observado of the payment day, but use your bank's real amount.
6. **If your bank moved it to pesos:** use **Transfer to CLP** and enter the peso charge exactly as it appears on your statement. You can undo it while that peso statement is unpaid.
