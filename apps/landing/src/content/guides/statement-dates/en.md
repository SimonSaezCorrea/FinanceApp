The closing date (fecha de facturación) is the day your bank ends the billing period and issues the statement with everything you bought since the previous closing. The payment due date (fecha de vencimiento) is the last day to pay that statement, and it always comes a few days after the closing. Anything you buy after the closing is not in that statement: it is charged in the next one.

## The two dates, simply put

- **Billing period** (período de facturación): the stretch of time, usually a month, during which your purchases add up.
- **Closing date** (fecha de facturación): the day that period ends and the **statement** (estado de cuenta) is issued. It is the monthly document with your purchases, interest, fees, the **total to pay** (total a pagar) and the **minimum payment** (pago mínimo).
- **Payment due date** (fecha de vencimiento): the day, set in your card contract, by which you must pay what the statement says.

Your issuer sets both dates in your card contract and prints them on every statement. There is no single date for every bank in Chile, so check yours.

## An example with dates and amounts

Say your bank closes on the **20th of each month** and gives you until the **5th of the following month** to pay.

| Date         | Purchase    | Amount      | Which statement is it on?   |
| ------------ | ----------- | ----------- | --------------------------- |
| September 25 | Supermarket | $45,990 CLP | The one closing October 20  |
| October 2    | Pharmacy    | $18,500 CLP | The one closing October 20  |
| October 14   | Bus tickets | $36,000 CLP | The one closing October 20  |
| October 22   | Gas         | $40,000 CLP | The one closing November 20 |

The statement closing on **October 20** adds up to **$100,490 CLP** and is due on **November 5**. The gas from October 22 missed that cut: it shows up on the November statement, due in December.

That is why a purchase made right after the closing takes the longest to be charged, while one made the day before the closing is due a little over two weeks later.

## Paying the total, the minimum or nothing

- **You pay the total by the due date**: with most cards you pay no interest on that period's single-payment purchases. Cash advances (avances) and purchases in installments (cuotas) have their own terms, set in your contract.
- **You pay only the minimum**: you are not late, but what you did not pay stays as debt and accrues revolving interest (intereses rotativos), which shows up on the next statement. Your debt takes longer to clear.
- **You pay nothing, or less than the minimum**: on top of the interest, late interest (intereses moratorios) and collection fees can be charged.

Your issuer calculates the minimum payment under rules set by Chilean regulation; it is printed on your statement.

## Where to find your real dates

On your card's **statement** (estado de cuenta): it shows the closing date, the due date, the total to pay and the minimum payment. They are also in your contract and usually in your bank's app or website.

If the due date falls on a non-business day, your contract says whether it moves to the next business day. The consumer information rules for credit cards in force today date from 2012 (Decreto 44); a new regulation (Decreto 75) has been published and takes effect on February 6, 2028, defining these dates in more detail.

The CMF (Comisión para el Mercado Financiero) is Chile's financial regulator, and SERNAC (Servicio Nacional del Consumidor) is the consumer protection agency.

## How to do it in _fik_

In _fik_ each statement uses the real dates of your bank statement, so what you see in the app matches what the bank charges:

1. Add your credit card under **Accounts**. The purchases you record add up in the open period.
2. When your statement arrives, open the card, go to **Statements** and tap **Generate statement**. Enter the start, closing and due dates exactly as they appear on the statement.
3. _fik_ keeps in that statement only what you bought up to the closing. Later purchases move to the next period, just like in the example.
4. When you pay, use **Pay** and choose the account the money comes from and how much: the total, the minimum (if you set your card's minimum payment percentage) or another amount. If you pay less than the total, the rest moves to the next statement.
5. To pay ahead before the closing, use **Prepay** on the open period.

Each statement shows its due date and is flagged as overdue if it passes unpaid.
