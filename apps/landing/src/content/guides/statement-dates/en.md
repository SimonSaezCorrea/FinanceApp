The closing date is the day your bank ends the billing period and issues the statement with everything you bought since the previous closing. Chilean regulation calls it the **statement billing date** (fecha de facturación del estado de cuenta).

The payment due date is the last day to pay that statement, and it always comes a few days after the closing. Regulation calls it the **due date or payment deadline** (fecha de vencimiento o límite para pagar).

Anything you buy after the closing is not on that statement. It is charged on the next one.

## The three concepts, simply put

- **Billing period (período de facturación):** the stretch of time, usually a month, during which your purchases add up.
- **Closing date (fecha de facturación):** the day that period ends and the **statement** (estado de cuenta) is issued, with your purchases, interest, fees, the **total to pay** (total a pagar) and the **minimum payment** (pago mínimo).
- **Payment due date (fecha de vencimiento):** the day set in your card contract by which you must pay what the statement says.

Your issuer sets both dates in your contract and prints them on every statement. There is no single date for every bank in Chile, so check yours.

## An example with dates and amounts

Say your bank closes on the **20th of each month** and gives you until the **5th of the following month** to pay.

| Date         | Purchase    | Amount      | Which statement is it on?   |
| ------------ | ----------- | ----------- | --------------------------- |
| September 25 | Supermarket | $45,990 CLP | The one closing October 20  |
| October 2    | Pharmacy    | $18,500 CLP | The one closing October 20  |
| October 14   | Bus tickets | $36,000 CLP | The one closing October 20  |
| October 22   | Gas         | $40,000 CLP | The one closing November 20 |

The statement closing on **October 20** adds up to **$100,490 CLP** and is due on **November 5**. The gas from October 22 missed that cut: it shows up on the November statement, due in December.

That is why a purchase made right after the closing takes the longest to be charged. One made the day before the closing is due a little over two weeks later.

## Total billed and total to pay

Your statement shows two totals that look alike but are not:

- **Total billed (monto total facturado):** everything billed in the period, including charges that have not accrued yet.
- **Total to pay (monto total a pagar):** only what has already accrued. It is the amount that brings you fully up to date.

## Paying the total, the minimum or nothing

- **You pay the total by the due date:** with most cards you pay no interest on that period's single-payment purchases. Cash advances (avances) and purchases in installments (cuotas) have their own terms, set in your contract.
- **You pay only the minimum:** you are not late, but what you did not pay stays as debt and accrues revolving interest (intereses rotativos). That interest shows up on the next statement and your debt takes longer to clear.
- **You pay nothing, or less than the minimum:** on top of the interest, late interest (intereses moratorios) and collection fees can be charged.

Your issuer calculates the minimum payment and prints it on your statement. By regulation, it cannot be lower than the interest that last month's unpaid debt accrued during the period, unless a promotion lets you skip payments for a while.

Keep in mind what that means: if you pay only the minimum every month, you may be paying mostly interest while your debt barely goes down.

## If the due date falls on a non-business day

If the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday, your contract must move it to the next business day or to a date no more than five calendar days later. The regulation requires it, unless all of your issuer's branches are open on non-business days.

## Which rules apply today

The credit card consumer information regulation in force today dates from 2012 (Decreto 44). The non-business-day rule and the minimum payment floor are already in it.

The new regulation (Decreto 75) was published in the Diario Oficial on August 6, 2026 and takes effect on February 6, 2028. It keeps both rules and defines each date and amount on the statement in more detail.

The CMF (Comisión para el Mercado Financiero) is Chile's financial regulator, and SERNAC (Servicio Nacional del Consumidor) is the consumer protection agency.

## Where to find your real dates

Your card's **statement** (estado de cuenta) shows the closing date, the due date, the total to pay and the minimum payment. They are also in your contract and usually in your bank's app or website.

## How to do it in _fik_

In _fik_ each statement uses the real dates of your bank statement. That way what you see in the app matches what the bank charges:

1. **Record your purchases:** add your credit card under **Accounts** and record your purchases. They add up in the open period.
2. **Get your statement:** when it arrives, find the closing date, the due date and the total to pay.
3. **Generate the statement with those dates:** open the card, go to **Statements** and tap **Generate statement**. Enter the start, closing and due dates exactly as printed; _fik_ keeps in it only what you bought up to the closing and moves later purchases to the next period.
4. **Record your payment or prepayment:** use **Pay** and choose the account the money comes from and how much: the total, the minimum (if you set your card's minimum payment percentage) or another amount. If you pay less than the total, the rest moves to the next statement. To pay ahead before the closing, use **Prepay** on the open period.

Each statement shows its due date and is flagged as overdue if it passes unpaid.
