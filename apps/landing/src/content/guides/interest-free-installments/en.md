An interest-free instalment purchase (compra en cuotas sin interés) takes the full amount off your credit limit on the day you buy, but each statement only charges that month's instalment. The limit is freed again as you pay each instalment.

If you pay the full billed amount every month, the purchase costs the same as paying cash. If you pay less, what you leave unpaid becomes debt that earns interest, even if it came from an interest-free instalment.

## The three concepts, simply put

- **Interest-free instalment (cuota sin interés):** an equal part of the purchase charged on each statement, with no interest added.
- **Available limit (cupo disponible):** what you have left to spend. An instalment purchase lowers it by the full amount at once, not instalment by instalment.
- **Minimum payment (pago mínimo):** the least you can pay on a statement without falling into arrears. Since June 2026 a CMF formula defines it, and interest-free instalments enter that formula gradually.

## An example with amounts

Say your card has a limit of **$1,000,000** (Chilean pesos) and you buy a fridge for **$600,000** in **6 interest-free instalments** of **$100,000**.

| When                       | Limit used | Limit available |
| -------------------------- | ---------- | --------------- |
| Before buying              | $0         | $1,000,000      |
| On the day of the purchase | $600,000   | $400,000        |
| 1st instalment paid        | $500,000   | $500,000        |
| 3rd instalment paid        | $300,000   | $700,000        |
| 6th and last paid          | $0         | $1,000,000      |

Each statement carries one **$100,000** instalment. If you pay the billed total, you end up paying **$600,000**, the same as cash.

## Why the limit drops all at once

Your bank lent you the $600,000 on the day of the purchase: it paid the shop in full. What it charges you in instalments is the repayment of that loan, so while you owe instalments, that part of the limit stays in use.

That is why, after several instalment purchases, you can have little limit left even though each statement is small.

## How they enter the minimum payment

Since 4 June 2026, the CMF (Chile's financial market regulator) requires the minimum payment to include 100% of the period's interest, fees and charges, plus at least 5% of the rest of what was billed. The rule calls the first part **non-financeable amounts** (montos no financiables) and the second **financeable amounts** (montos financiables).

The rule comes from Ley 21.673, a law to reduce and prevent over-indebtedness. Interest-free instalments move from "the rest" into the part paid in full in five stages. For the $100,000 instalment in the example, if it were the only thing on your statement:

| From          | Share of the instalment paid in full | Minimum payment for that instalment |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| June 2026     | 0%                                   | $5,000                              |
| December 2026 | 25%                                  | $28,750                             |
| June 2027     | 50%                                  | $52,500                             |
| December 2027 | 75%                                  | $76,250                             |
| June 2028     | 100%                                 | $100,000                            |

From June 2028, the minimum payment includes the whole interest-free instalment. You can no longer pay only part of it without falling into arrears.

Every row is worked out the same way. For example, from December 2026: 25% of the instalment ($25,000) plus 5% of the remaining 75% ($3,750) makes $28,750.

## Paying the minimum with interest-free instalments

Paying the minimum keeps you up to date, but what you did not pay of the billed total stays as debt and earns your card's interest. In the example, if this month you pay $5,000 of the $100,000 instalment, the remaining $95,000 starts earning interest.

If you want the purchase to stay interest-free, pay the full billed amount every month.

## Before buying in interest-free instalments

- **Compare with the cash price:** shops sometimes discount paying cash (al contado). If they do, the interest-free instalments cost you more.
- **Check the limit you have left:** the full purchase stays in use until you pay the last instalment.
- **Check the conditions:** the number of interest-free instalments and any fee depend on the promotion and on your bank.

## How to do it in _fik_

In _fik_ a credit card instalment purchase works as it does at your bank: it takes the full amount off the limit and each statement charges the instalment that falls in it.

1. **Create the plan:** under **Installments**, tap **New plan**. Enter the total amount, the number of instalments and the date of the first one, pick the card in **Payment card** and leave **Interest per period** at 0.
2. **Check your limit:** from that day, the card's used limit includes the whole purchase.
3. **Generate the statement:** when you generate each statement with your bank statement's dates, the instalment due in that period is marked **Billed** and added to the total.
4. **Pay the statement:** once paid, the instalment is marked paid and its share of the limit is freed. If you pay less than the total, the rest moves to the next statement.

The plan shows how many instalments are billed, how many are left and what remains to pay.
