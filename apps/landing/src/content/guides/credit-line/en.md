A credit line (línea de crédito) is an agreed overdraft on your checking account (cuenta corriente): it lets your balance go below zero up to an amount you agreed with the bank. A credit card is a separate product, with its own credit limit and a monthly statement with a due date.

The big difference is interest. The credit line charges from the day your balance goes negative; with a credit card, if you pay the total on time, most cards charge no interest on single-payment purchases.

## The two, simply put

- **Credit line (línea de crédito):** credit attached to your checking account. It has no card and no statement of its own: it kicks in by itself when you pay or withdraw more than you have.
- **Credit card (tarjeta de crédito):** a payment method with its own credit limit. Its purchases add up on a monthly statement, which you pay by the due date.

## How the credit line charges interest

Interest runs only from the day the account goes negative. It cannot be charged for days before that debit.

The bank charges it for past periods of at least 30 days, usually at the end of the month, and when it makes the charge it must send you a notice with the interest amount. The rate is variable and subject to Chile's maximum conventional rate (tasa máxima convencional).

## How the credit line is paid

Since 2020, Ley 21.167 makes the credit line pay itself off: every deposit or transfer that reaches your checking account goes first to the credit line's debt.

You can ask the bank not to do it automatically, at any time, and the change applies from the following month. Then you pay the line yourself with deposits or transfers. Charges are calculated the same way either way.

If you spend beyond the agreed line, that is an **unagreed overdraft** (sobregiro no pactado). The bank does not have to accept it: the rules treat it as an exception, so it may decline the payment.

If it accepts it, the first deposits that reach the account pay that excess first.

## An example with amounts

Say you have $50,000 CLP in your checking account and a credit line of $300,000 CLP.

| Day        | Movement                       | Account balance | Credit line used |
| ---------- | ------------------------------ | --------------- | ---------------- |
| October 3  | You pay $200,000 CLP           | −$150,000 CLP   | $150,000 CLP     |
| October 15 | Your $900,000 CLP salary lands | $750,000 CLP    | $0               |

From October 3 to 15 you used $150,000 CLP of the line, and those days accrue interest. The salary paid off the line by itself when it arrived.

Had you made that same $200,000 CLP purchase with a credit card, it would go on the statement. Paying the total by the due date, most cards would charge no interest on it.

## Which one to use

- **For a gap of a few days:** the line covers the negative balance and the next deposit pays it off, but every day below zero costs interest.
- **For the month's purchases:** the card gives you until the due date without interest, if you pay the total.
- **For long-term debt:** neither the line nor the card's minimum payment is meant for that. Compare their rates, shown in your contract and your account statement (cartola), with those of a consumer loan (crédito de consumo) too.

A credit line has a maximum amount, a term and an interest rate agreed in your contract. Any increase in the amount needs your consent.

## How to do it in _fik_

In _fik_ the credit line is part of your checking account, and the credit card is an account of its own:

1. **Add your credit line:** when you create your checking account under **Accounts**, open **More details** and enter the amount of your **Overdraft line**. You can also add it later by editing the account. The balance can then go below zero down to that amount, and an expense that goes past it is rejected, just like at the bank.
2. **Record your movements:** when the balance is negative, you are using the line. Every income you record brings it back toward zero, just like the automatic payment.
3. **Record the interest:** when the bank charges the line's interest, record it as an expense on the checking account, with the amount in the bank's notice.
4. **Add your card separately:** create the credit card as an account of type **Credit card**, with its limit, its statements and its due date.
